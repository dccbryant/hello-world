Statements that may conflict

Pairs of the subject's own clauses that share most of their words but disagree in polarity or in a number. Read each pair in context; the pattern catches restatements and corrections as readily as inconsistencies.

What this does and does not show. Every measure here rises with cognitive load, arousal or discomfort. None of them is specific to lying. Blink rate also rises with dry air and contact lenses; eyes move down when people retrieve memories; pitch rises when people are nervous for any reason; hedging is also what careful, truthful people do. The control baseline matters more than any single figure, so start the interview with questions the subject can answer easily and truthfully. Read the amber as a map of where the conversation became effortful for the subject. No single physiological or linguistic signal has been shown to detect deception reliably, and combined ones do only modestly better than chance in the published literature. Recording a call, by screen capture or otherwise, may require the other participants' consent; the rules depend on where each of you is.
